    Make your move.

    Tap a piece, then a marked square. Capture the other king’s defenders and give checkmate: the king is in check with no legal escape.

    • White moves first. Pawns move one square forward (or two from their starting square) and capture one square diagonally.
    • Knights jump in an L. Bishops go diagonally; rooks go straight. Queens do both. Kings move one square.
    • To castle, select your king and move it two squares toward the rook. Your king must be safe along the way.
    • En passant: immediately after an enemy pawn moves two squares past your pawn, you can capture it diagonally as if it moved one square. The marked destination handles this for you.
    • A pawn reaching the far end becomes your choice of queen, rook, bishop or knight.

    Dots show moves; rings show captures. A checked king has a red outline and a CHECK label. Last move squares have corner marks.

    Keyboard: Tab to the board, arrows to a square, Enter/Space to select or move, Escape to clear. Review history with the arrow buttons; return to Live board to play.

    Hints and takebacks are optional practice aids. Chess has draws too: stalemate or insufficient material ends play. Three repetitions or 50 moves per side without a pawn move/capture allow a claim; five repetitions or 75 moves end play automatically. Claim draw also offers legal intended moves that qualify.
